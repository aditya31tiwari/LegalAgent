Standard Single-Clause LLM (Failure Mode)

Why Isolated Analysis Fails

When an LLM reviews Clause 91.1 ("Aggregate liability capped at ₹1,000") in isolation, it simply summarizes it as "Standard limitation of liability clause". When it reviews Clause 91.2 ("Concessionaire shall indemnify without financial limitation"), it notes "Standard indemnity". It never notices that the two clauses destroy each other's legal validity unless given the full multi-hundred page context simultaneously.

LegalAgent Cross-Clause GraphRAG (Solution)

How Graph Topology Catches Contradictions

LegalAgent extracts clauses into a directed relational graph G=(V,E). Candidate selection links Clause 91.1 and 91.2 via our TYPE_MATRIX rule (Liability × Indemnity). The cross-clause analyzer verifies the contradiction, cites Sections 124–125 ICA, and flags a CRITICAL CONFLICT with zero hallucination.

Clause 91.1

Illusory Limitation of Liability

Detected in Demo Critical Conflict
"The aggregate liability of the Authority shall not exceed INR 1,000 for any claim, including fraud, death, personal injury, gross negligence, and statutory penalties."
Indian Law Grounding
Section 23, Indian Contract Act, 1872: Any clause seeking to exclude or limit liability for one's own intentional fraud, wilful misconduct, or causing death/personal injury is unlawful and contrary to public policy.
Cross-Clause Detection Nexus
Clashes directly with Clause 91.2 (Uncapped Indemnity) and the Concessionaire's revenue framework. Flagged in dashboard with red conflict edge.
Clause 91.2

Unlimited Indemnity Override

Detected in Demo Conflict Nexus
"The Concessionaire shall defend, indemnify, and hold harmless the Authority against every claim, loss, fine, and expense without any financial limitation and notwithstanding Clause 91.1."
Indian Law Grounding
Sections 124 & 125, ICA 1872: Indemnity is an independent primary contract obligation. Uncapped indemnities without explicit coordination carveouts against liability ceilings create perpetual arbitral dispute.
Cross-Clause Detection Nexus
Retrieved against Clause 91.1 via TYPE_MATRIX rule. Engine detects the uncoordinated "notwithstanding" clash and alerts counsel to insert a proper liability carveout.
Clause 91.3

Post-Termination Non-Compete Restraint

Statutory Void Ab Initio Detected in Demo
"For 36 months following termination, the Concessionaire shall not compete with, advise, or participate in any competing infrastructure business in India."
Indian Law Grounding
Section 27, Indian Contract Act, 1872: Supreme Court Constitution & regular benches (Percept D'Mark v. Zaheer Khan (2006) 4 SCC 227, Niranjan Shankar Golikari (1967)): All post-termination covenants in restraint of trade are void ab initio. No reasonableness exception exists.
Detection Mechanism
Flagged deterministically by analyse_statutory(). Recommends complete deletion and substitution with valid trade secret non-disclosure covenants.
Clause 91.4

Perpetual Survival of Void Restraints

Detected in Demo Dependency Risk
"Clauses 91.2 and 91.3 shall survive termination indefinitely and remain enforceable after termination of this Agreement."
Indian Law Grounding
Coupled with Clause 91.3, this extends an already void restraint indefinitely. Courts will sever the entire survival covenant with respect to the non-compete.
Cross-Clause Detection Nexus
Detected by termination_survival_dependency detector in pipeline. Flags cross-reference link to Clause 91.3.
Clause 91.5

Punitive Liquidated Damages Penalty

Planned Detector Section 74 ICA
"A delay of one day shall incur automatic liquidated damages equal to 75 percent of the total concession value, regardless of actual loss."
Indian Law Grounding
Section 74, Indian Contract Act, 1872 & Kailash Nath Associates v. DDA (2015): Agreed sums cannot be purely punitive. Forfeiture of 75% for 1 day delay is a penalty in terrorem; courts will only grant reasonable compensation upon evidence of actual injury.
Roadmap Status
Implemented in seeded vendor_supply contract. Scheduled for inclusion in generic CLI regex detector in Sprint 2.
Clause 91.6

Contractual Cap on DPDPA Statutory Penalties

Detected in Demo DPDPA 2023 Violation
"Any penalty imposed under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 shall be limited to INR 10,000 between the parties."
Indian Law Grounding
Digital Personal Data Protection Act (DPDPA), 2023: Statutory penalties levied by the Data Protection Board of India reach up to ₹250 Crore. These are public-law regulatory sanctions that cannot be contracted away or capped between private parties.
Detection in Dashboard
Seeded in pune_metro_ppp and tech_msa. Surfaces as High-Severity Statutory Violation citing DPDPA 2023 Schedule penalties.
Clause 91.7

Commercial Passenger Data Monetization Without Consent

Planned Detector DPDPA Section 6
"The Concessionaire may collect, sell, and process all passenger personal data for any commercial purpose without notice or affirmative consent."
Indian Law Grounding
Section 6, DPDPA 2023: Consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional, and unambiguous. Blanket authorization to sell passenger travel telemetry without explicit opt-in notice invites maximum statutory fines.
Roadmap Status
Targeted for DPDPA statutory analyzer enhancement in Phase 2 fine-tuning pipeline.
Clause 91.8

Unlawful Exclusion of Pre-Litigation Mediation

Planned Detector Mediation Act 2023
"No party shall attempt pre-litigation mediation and all disputes shall proceed directly to unilateral arbitration."
Indian Law Grounding
The Mediation Act, 2023: Mandates pre-litigation mediation for commercial disputes before approaching courts or arbitral tribunals, unless urgent interim protection is sought under Section 9 of the Arbitration Act.
Roadmap Status
Seeded in tech_msa finding f3. Automated keyword regex currently in development.
Clause 91.9

Repealed Penal Code Reference (IPC 420 vs BNS 318)

Planned Detector BNS 2023 Temporal Trap
"A breach shall be treated as cheating under Section 420 of the Indian Penal Code, even after the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita comes into force."
Indian Law Grounding
Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023 (enforced July 1, 2024): IPC is repealed. The offence of cheating is now governed by Section 318(4) BNS. Contractual clauses attempting to freeze repealed penal provisions create interpretive anomalies in criminal complaints.
Roadmap Status
Documented in research/06_FINE_TUNING_AND_TEMPORAL_MODEL_PLAN.md as part of our temporal era adapter.
Clause 91.10

Unilateral Commercial Amendment Power

Planned Detector Unilateral Discretion
"The Authority may amend any commercial term, fee, or risk allocation without notice or consent from the Concessionaire."
Indian Law Grounding
Section 62, Indian Contract Act, 1872: Contract novation and alteration require consensus ad idem of both parties. Unilateral amendment clauses in government contracts have been set aside under Article 14 for arbitrariness.
Roadmap Status
To be detected via mutual consent / unilateral keyword dependency checker.
Clause 91.11

Asymmetric Assignment Contradiction

Planned Detector Asymmetry
"The Concessionaire shall not assign this Agreement, but the Authority may freely assign all obligations to any third party without consent."
Indian Law Grounding
Standard infrastructure agreements allow Concessionaire assignment to project lenders for financing substitution. Complete unilateral assignment without lender rights breaches standard concession practice.
Roadmap Status
Candidate selection pair: assignment tags.
Clause 91.12

Absolute Statutory Warranty Disclaimer

Planned Detector Section 23 ICA
"The Authority gives no warranty whatsoever, including for title, legality, safety, or fitness, and the Concessionaire waives every statutory remedy."
Indian Law Grounding
Section 28, ICA 1872: Agreements extinguishing party rights or barring legal recourse within the statutory limitation period are void. Blanket statutory waivers of title and fundamental statutory duties are unenforceable.
Roadmap Status
Extends analyse_statutory() with Section 28 limitation/remedy-extinguishment rules.
Clause 91.13

Prohibition Against Stamp Duty Impounding

Planned Detector Maharashtra Stamp Act
"The parties agree that this concession is intentionally unstamped and that no party may seek impounding or cure of stamp duty."
Indian Law Grounding
Supreme Court 7-Judge Bench (Dec 2023, In Re Interplay Between Arbitration Agreements and Stamp Act): Unstamped agreements are not void ab initio, but mandatory impounding under the Stamp Act is a matter of public revenue that parties cannot contractually prohibit.
Roadmap Status
Seeded in vendor_supply finding fv2.
Clause 91.14

Perpetual Public Safety Gag Order

Planned Detector Public Policy & RTI
"Every operational fact, public filing, safety report, and statutory disclosure shall remain confidential forever and may never be disclosed to any regulator."
Indian Law Grounding
Concessionaires on public transport projects are subject to Commissioner of Metro Railway Safety (CMRS) mandatory disclosure under the Metro Railways Act. Confidentiality clauses gagging statutory regulators are unlawful under Section 23 ICA.
Roadmap Status
Confidentiality carveout detector scheduled for Phase 2.
Clause 91.15

Foreign Seat Override in Purely Domestic Concession

Planned Detector Arbitration Act 1996
"This Agreement shall be governed by the laws of England and disputes shall be decided exclusively by courts in London notwithstanding the Indian project and Indian governing approvals."
Indian Law Grounding
Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996: Two Indian entities entering into a domestic infrastructure concession agreement in India cannot derogate from Indian substantive law or opt out of Indian jurisdiction to defeat mandatory provisions of Indian law.
Roadmap Status
Governing law vs jurisdiction clash detector.
